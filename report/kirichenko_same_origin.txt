Same Origin, Different Verdicts: Text Production Conditions
Confound Machine-Generated Text Detection in Russian
Stanislav Kirichenko
Independent Researcher
staurus86@gmail.com
ORCID: 0009-0001-2914-9541
Abstract
Automated assessment of whether a text was machine-generated is applied where the
verdict carries a cost for the author, yet origin is a property of the production process, not
an observable property of the text. The conditions under which a text was produced — how
rigid the task was, the editorial rules, the platform template — push it in the same direction
in which a generative model is presumed to push it.
This study measures the contribution of these conditions while origin is held fixed. The
Russian-language corpus of 1882 documents comprises a machine part, generated by four
generation channels — each backed by its own model family — across 45 tasks in three
task modes, and an archival human part annotated for source regimentation level before
the texts were read. The primary contrast is computed within the machine corpus: both
halves of a pair are generated by the same model for the same task and differ only in task
wording. Machine-likeness is measured by four operationalisations — an interpretable index
with weights fixed before data collection, a trained classifier, a zero-shot metric on an open
model, and a judge model.
The main result is disagreement between procedures. Changing the task mode shifted
the scores of all four, but the direction did not match: three gave a negative shift, the judge
a positive one, and none of the four intervals covers zero. The cross-procedure comparison
plan and the specifications of procedures 2–4 were fixed after the result of procedure 1 had
been obtained but before procedures 2–4 were run; the disagreement survived correction of a
corpus defect and of the nested validation.
Two observations explain why such scores cannot be read as measurements of authorship.
A baseline on a single feature — the source identifier — reaches a median AUROC of 1.0000
across the thirteen group splits where AUROC is defined, meaning that the origin class is
confounded with how the corpus was assembled. Removing emphasis markers did not change
the full text by a single character, yet it moved the boundary between heading and prose,
changed the format counters, and flipped a confident machine verdict to human for every
applicable model.
The scores should be interpreted as properties of a specific measurement system and corpus,
not as a direct measurement of text origin. The system distinguishes not only linguistic style
but traces of data collection, repetition, formatting, and preprocessing decisions.
Keywords:AI-generated text detection; Russian-language text; production conditions; source
confounding; preprocessing sensitivity.
1 Introduction
Why this matters
Automated assessment of text origin is applied where the decision carries a cost: checking student
work, moderating platforms, screening contractors, editorial policies. A verdict of ‘machine-
1
written’ carries consequences for the author, and so the question of what exactly a detector
measures stops being a technical one.
The cost of error is asymmetric. A missed machine text is cheap; a false accusation against
a human is expensive, and the author cannot defend against it: proving one’s own authorship
is harder than disputing it. In the accusatory scenario, therefore, what decides is not average
accuracy but the detector’s behaviour at a low false positive rate — and on this characteristic
systems diverge dramatically.
Machine-likeness is not directly observable
The origin of a text is a property of the process, not of the text itself. We observe only the result,
and any machine-likeness score is an operationalisation: a set of features, a way of weighting
them, a decision threshold.
An uncomfortable consequence follows: different operationalisations may diverge, and this is
not necessarily an error on the part of either. Until they are shown to measure the same thing,
the question ‘which detector is right’ is posed before it can be answered.
This property of the process depends not only on who wrote the text but also on the conditions
under which it was written. A rigid technical specification narrows the choice of wording; editorial
regimentation standardises the structure; a platform template dictates the formatting. All three
push the text in the same direction in which the machine is presumed to act.
How authorship is confounded in practice
A corpus for detector evaluation is almost always assembled so that the origin class coincides
with something else.
Human texts are taken from platforms, machine texts are generated through channels — and
the source becomes a proxy for the class. Human texts come from genres where one regimentation
regime applies, machine texts are written to tasks of a different kind — and the genre becomes a
proxy for the class. Human texts keep the publication’s formatting, machine texts arrive from an
API without it — and the formatting becomes a proxy for the class. Finally, both classes pass
through a data preparation pipeline, and its decisions — what counts as a heading, what counts
as prose — change the model input without changing a single word.
Each of these confounds is known in isolation. What happens to a machine-likeness score
when production conditions change while origin stays the same is a separate question, and it is
rarely asked.
What the literature review established
A systematic sample of 73 papers shows that studies predominantly evaluate detector accuracy
and robustness to editing, while the human side of the comparison is assembled opportunistically,
without control over the task, the regimentation, or the production channel.
In this sample, no studies were found that separately measure the effect of production
conditions on machine-likeness scores with origin held fixed. Nor were any estimates found of
the false positive rate for formal and translated Russian-language prose at the same time. Both
claims are narrowed to the scope of the search performed.
Design
The study compares production conditions under fixed tasks.
The machine part of the corpus was generated by four generation channels — each backed by
its own model family — on 45 tasks in three modes: from a neutral technical specification to
a task requiring the avoidance of typical machine-text features, with two replicates. The main
contrast is computed within the machine corpus: both halves of a pair are generated by the same
2
model for the same task, and only the formulation differs. Origin does not change; the conditions
do.
The human part consists of archival texts published before 2022, annotated by source regi-
mentation level before the texts themselves were read. It includes the stratum on which the cost
of error is measured: formal academic prose and translations.
Machine-likeness is measured by four procedures with different inputs and different provenance
of weights: an interpretable index with weights fixed before data collection; a trained classifier
on the same feature matrix with group splits; a zero-shot metric on an open Russian-language
model; a judge model with a rubric. None is declared the gold standard.
The design, hypotheses, and analysis rules were fixed by preregistration before the dependent
variable was computed; deviations are published as amendments with dates and reasons.
Research questions
1. Do text production conditions change its machine-likeness score under a fixed task?
2. Do the direction and magnitude of the effect agree across the four operationalisations?
3. How strongly do the results depend on the source, genre, register, formatting, and transfor-
mations of the text?
Contributions
The study delivers four things.
A measurement of the effect of conditions with origin held fixed— on a Russian-
language corpus, with a fixed task and a preregistered contrast.
A test of the agreement of the four operationalisationsinstead of an assumption
of it, with an explicit rule: matching signs count as convergence of operationalisations, not as
independent replication.
An assessment of how the result depends on conditions that usually stay in the
background— the source, genre, formal register, formatting, and ten transformations of the
text, including cases where the decision flips while the text remains unchanged.
Recorded limitations of transferability— which of the reported numbers do not transfer
to applications where both classes come from a single source, and why.
The study does not propose a new detector and does not improve existing ones. Its subject is
what the procedures already in use actually measure when text production conditions change
while origin remains the same.
2 Research gap
1. What the literature measures
Research predominantly evaluates detector accuracy and robustness to editing.
Two findings in this area are firmly established. The first: a universal binary detector transfers
poorly to new models, domains, and attacks — RAID assembled six million generations across
eleven models and eight domains and showed performance collapsing under a change of decoding
strategy and under adversarial attacks (Dugan et al. 2024); on unfamiliar domains and generators,
TPR at 1% FPR falls as low as zero (Tufts et al. 2025); M4 (Wang et al. 2024a) and MULTITuDE
across eleven languages (Macko et al. 2023) give the same result. The second: average accuracy is
unfit for the accusatory scenario — in an audit of four detectors on 1992 excerpts, open systems
mistook up to 78% of human text for machine text, and at an FPR ceiling of 0.5% only one
commercial system retained detection (Jabarian and Imas 2025).
3
Borile and Abrate 2025 name the cause of poor transfer: linguistic and domain confounders
create spurious correlations that do not carry beyond the training distribution. The question of
which specific conditions give rise to these correlations is not posed there.
2. How the human side of the comparison is assembled
The human reference is often collected opportunistically — from whatever is available — without
control over the task, the regimentation, or the production channel.
This is not a complaint about individual studies but a consequence of how the field is set
up: to measure a detector, any corpus of demonstrably human texts will do, and ready-made
collections are the most convenient choice. Under such selection, the conditions in which these
texts were written are not recorded and do not enter the analysis.
The cost is visible in Fredrick and Craven 2025: the direction of differences in lexical diversity
changes with the composition of the human control group. If a machine-likeness score depends on
the conditions under which the reference was written, then a threshold calibrated on one corpus
carries over to another with no guarantees.
3. What the sample lacks
Within the included sample of 73 studies, no research was found that separately
assessed how text production conditions — task mode, regimentation level, and
generation channel — change a text’s machine-likeness scores while its origin remains
unchanged.
Three groups of studies come close, and each stops short. Studies of editing prevalence count
how much text has passed through a model (Liang et al. 2024, Kobak et al. 2025) but do not ask
how much machine-likeness the task itself creates. Work on hybrid authorship varies the mode of
editing (Zeng et al. 2024, Artemova et al. 2025, Su et al. 2025, Baumler et al. 2026) — yet the
human reference in these studies is written freely, without regimentation. Fairness studies show
that the score varies across subgroups of writers (Liang et al. 2023, Stowe et al. 2026, Basu et al.
2026), not across the conditions under which the text was produced.
CoATcomesclosest—aRussian-languagecorpusof246thousandtextsandthirteengenerators
(Shamardina et al. 2024). It shows directly that the mode of production changes detectability:
machine-translated texts are recognised with an accuracy of 0.621, while free generation is
recognised with 0.99. The study stops at the same point as the others: the generator changes
together with the task type, so the effect of conditions is not separated from the effect of origin,
and the human side is drawn from the same domains without control over task or regimentation.
Close by stands the study by Gritsay and colleagues (Gritsay et al. 2023): detecting
machine-generated fragments in Russian-language scientific documents yields an F1 of 0.968
with multi-domain training and paraphrase augmentation, against 0.825 in English. The authors
conclude outright that a universal detector for all domains and languages is unlikely to be
achievable — a conclusion that matches ours in substance and was obtained on different data.
There, too, production conditions are not varied with origin held unchanged.
To this is added a language gap. The matrix contains five Russian-language primary studies,
and none varies production conditions with origin unchanged: RuATD collects generators
(Shamardina et al. 2022), LLMTrace annotates mixed authorship character by character (Tolstykh
et al. 2025), AINL-Eval measures transfer to unseen scientific fields (Batura et al. 2025), CoAT
compares generative task types together with a change of generator (Shamardina et al. 2024), and
the study by Gritsay and colleagues measures transfer between domains of scientific documents
(Gritsay et al. 2023).
Among the included studies, no FPR estimates were found that cover formal and
translated Russian-language prose at the same time.
4
Both claims are worded to stay within the scope of the search performed: they speak of what
is absent from the included sample, not of what is absent from the literature at large.
Where the sample is thinner, this is stated outright.A targeted follow-up search on 1

both were published outside arXiv and outside international conferences. The original protocol
searched mostly by English keywords in English-language databases, so sample completeness for

4. What the present study does
This study closes the gap by comparing production conditions under fixed tasks and with several
non-coinciding operationalisations of machine-likeness.
The task is held fixed while its mode varies — from a rigid technical specification to a free
formulation. Origin does not change: both halves of the compared pair are produced by the same
model from the same task. The human part is annotated by source regimentation level, and the
annotation is done from the source before the texts are read.
Machine-likeness is measured by four procedures with different inputs and different ways
of obtaining weights: an interpretable index with weights set before data collection; a trained
classifier on the same feature matrix; a zero-shot metric on an open Russian-language model; and
a judge model scoring against a rubric. None is treated as a gold standard, and the agreement of
their signs is tested rather than assumed.
What remains background in the studies listed above is measured separately: FPR on the
formal register and translated prose, stability of the decision under text transformations, and the
contribution of source and genre compared with the contribution of style.
A limitation of the method is named upfront: word lists have a shelf life, because authors adapt
to published markers (Geng and Trotta 2025). This is an argument for features not reducible to
a word list, and it was taken into account in feature selection.
3 Related work
1. The detection task and the limits of its solution
A universal binary detector does not transfer beyond the conditions in which it was tested. RAID
showed that detectors break under a change of decoding strategy and under adversarial edits
(Dugan et al. 2024). A direct test of seven systems yields TPR at FPR 1% down to zero on
unfamiliar domains and generators (Tufts et al. 2025); the same result is reproduced multilingually
(Macko et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024a; Macko et al. 2025). Borile and Abrate 2025 name the
cause: the detector picks up linguistic and domain confounders, and these correlations do not
transfer.
Estimates of how far the task is solved at all diverge: 99.0 F1 cross-domain (Verma et al.
2024) on the aggregate against drops to zero (Tufts et al. 2025) and to 35% under attacks (Wang
et al. 2024b) once the estimate is broken down by condition. Both groups are right within their
own design, and this is the first thing that gets in the way of comparing results across papers.
A recurring design limitation: the human side of the comparison is almost always collected
opportunistically. The corpus is taken from ready sources — news, essays, Wikipedia — and the
machine part is generated to match it. The conditions under which the human text was written
are neither fixed nor varied.
Hence the first step towards our question: if detector quality changes with domain and
generator, the next question is whether it changes with the conditions under which the human
was writing.
5
2. Interpretable features and neural detectors
The line-up of methods is set by three classes: zero-shot detectors on likelihood statistics, from
probability curvature to the uniformity and diversity of information density (Mitchell et al. 2023;
Bao et al. 2024; Hans et al. 2024; Venkatraman et al. 2024; Basani et al. 2025); trained classifiers
(Verma et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2025; Mady et al. 2026); and interpretable features — the main
one for us.
The feature-based approach works and requires no access to the model. On American Chemical
Society papers, a classifier on off-the-shelf features separates human text from GPT-3.5 with
accuracy above 99% (Desaire et al. 2023). Stylometric, lexical, syntactic and phonological
features discriminate the classes across genres and models (Tulchinskii et al. 2023; Georgiou 2025;
Reinhart et al. 2025; Zamaraeva et al. 2025; Bakhshi 2026; Przystalski et al. 2026; Vera-Jimenez
et al. 2026), and the diversity measures they rely on have been validated separately (Covington
and McFall 2010; McCarthy and Jarvis 2010).
Which features are transferable is where studies diverge. A test of 284 features on 27 models
and ten domains left a single class stable — measures of lexical density (El Attar et al. 2026).
Studies that selected features within one type of text report high quality on that same type
(Georgiou 2025, Przystalski et al. 2026) — transferability they did not test.
A recurring design limitation: a feature is validated on class separation, not on stability across
production conditions. A paper reports that the feature separates human from machine, and
almost never what a change of genre, regimentation or the writer’s level of preparation does to it.
For our task the second point matters more. A feature that reverses direction with the
composition of the comparison is unfit for the role of a universal threshold, and the question of
what exactly it measures remains open.
3. Hybrid and edited authorship
Human editing breaks detection harder than a change of model. On 6.5 thousand expert-edited
texts, an expert’s edit moves machine text away from detection, while text edited by a model is
not recognised as human (Artemova et al. 2025). Under word-level and hybrid co-authorship,
segmentation and attribution degrade, in large part because almost half of the texts have a mean
homogeneous span of three sentences or less (Abassy et al. 2024; Devatine and Abraham 2024;
Richburg et al. 2024; Zeng et al. 2024; Su et al. 2025).
What editing does to style is where assessments diverge. A preregistered experiment with
81 participants showed a shift towards the writer’s own style (g=0.55) — and at the same time
greater retained closeness to the model than to the same person’s unassisted text (g=−1.43), plus
a rise in stylistic homogeneity (g=1.42) (Baumler et al. 2026). That is, editing simultaneously
brings the text closer to its author and leaves it machine-like by measurement; the conclusion
depends on which of the two quantities is taken as the target.
A recurring design limitation: the mode of editing is varied, but not the conditions of the
original writing. The human reference in these studies is written freely — the participant writes
as they wish, with no brief, no mandatory sections and no length requirement. CoAuthor records
interaction traces (Lee et al. 2022), but not regimentation.
Here the gap is most clearly visible. The literature measures what editing does to a text, and
does not measure what the task does to it.
4. Attacks and robustness
Evading a detector is cheap. Paraphrasing through DIPPER lowers DetectGPT accuracy from
70.3% to 4.6%, while a defence by retrieval over a base of 15 million generations recovers 80–
97% at FPR 1% on human texts (Krishna et al. 2023); homoglyphs, recursive paraphrasing,
humanisation and character-level edits give the same picture (Sadasivan et al. 2023; Huang et al.
2024; Wang et al. 2024b; Creo and Pudasaini 2025; Gu et al. 2026).
6
Whether this can be fixed is where conclusions diverge. Some studies propose architectural
remedies and show gains under attack (Huang et al. 2024; Bao et al. 2025; Ren et al. 2026);
others record that with an FPR ceiling of 0.5%, only one of four tested systems retains detection
(Jabarian and Imas 2025). The disagreement is explained not by method but by the FPR at
which quality is measured.
A recurring design limitation: the attack is modelled as a hostile action after the text is
written. Editing to evade and editing for quality are not separated in these studies, though
mechanically they differ: the former targets the detector, the latter the reader.
Our question lies nearby, but in a different plane. Regimentation, a template and a length
requirement change the text before it is produced, with no intent whatsoever to deceive a detector,
and in the terms of these studies such editing is not an attack at all.
5. Fairness and the cost of a false positive
Detector error is unevenly distributed across groups of writers. Detectors systematically assign
English texts by non-native speakers to the machine class while classifying native speakers’ texts
correctly (Liang et al. 2023; Stowe et al. 2026), and recall on texts of under-represented groups
is measured as a separate benchmark (Basu et al. 2026). An audit of four detectors on 1992
excerpts from six everyday genres: open systems mistake up to 78% of human text for machine
text, and with an FPR ceiling of 0.5% only one commercial system retains detection (Jabarian
and Imas 2025).
Assessments of human judgement diverge — from a single error by five prepared annotators
on 300 articles (Russell et al. 2025) to 31.5% correct identification in a web survey (Zaitsu et al.
2025); the accuracy of human judgement about origin depends strongly on the conditions of the
experiment. Separately, the ‘AI’ label moves human evaluation more than the text itself (Zhu et
al. 2024).
A recurring design limitation: inequality is sought along attributes of the writer — language
status, demographics, dialect. Writing conditions do not enter this grid, though formal register
and a rigid template are available to any author regardless of group.
For our work this is the nearest neighbour. If the machine-likeness score rises with regimenta-
tion, then part of the measured subgroup effect may be explained not by the group but by the
conditions under which that group usually writes.
6. Evaluation of style transfer and editing
The toolkit for evaluating edits is less developed than the editing itself: human evaluation
protocols are under-specified and not standardised (Briakou et al. 2021), only a few automatic
metrics have been validated in experiments with humans (Ostheimer et al. 2023; Mukherjee et al.
2025), content preservation metrics do not abstract away from the change of style (Pauli et al.
2025), and the very separability of style and content is contested (Jafaritazehjani et al. 2020).
A recurring design limitation: the target style is defined as a class — polite, formal, non-toxic.
Style as a consequence of an external requirement — a template, a mandatory set of sections, a
length limit — is not considered in these settings.
Hence a methodological foothold for our work and at the same time a caution: style shift can
be measured, but the metric must be chosen with an eye to the fact that its validity has not been
tested everywhere.
7. What of this has been tested on Russian
Beyond English, multilingual shared tasks and benchmarks cover from 11 to 22 languages (Macko
et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2024c, Macko et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2025). The sample contains
five primary Russian-language studies. RuATD 2022 put binary and multi-class detection of 14
7
generators into a shared task (Shamardina et al. 2022). LLMTrace annotates mixed authorship
character by character for English and Russian (Tolstykh et al. 2025). AINL-Eval 2025 provided
52,305 Russian scientific abstracts, five generators, and a requirement of transfer to unseen fields
and models (Batura et al. 2025).
The fourth study stands closest to the subject of the present research. CoAT is a corpus
of 246 thousand human and artificial texts, thirteen generators, six domains from the Russian
National Corpus to digital diaries (Shamardina et al. 2024). A transformer detector achieves
accuracy 0.864 there, humans on the same data — 0.66, generator attribution among fourteen
candidates — macro-F1 0.521. Transfer to unseen models and domains does not hold.
What matters for us is another dimension of the same study:detectability depends
strongly on the type of the generative task.Machine-translated texts are recognised with
accuracy 0.621, paraphrase — 0.768, free generation — 0.99. That is, the way a text was produced
shifts recognition accuracy from 0.621 to 0.99.
CoAT stops in the same place as the rest. The generator changes together with the task type,
so the effect of production conditions is not separated from the effect of origin. The human part
is collected from the same six domains with no control of task, regimentation or channel. The
authors name the limitations themselves: no diversity of decoding strategies and a risk of corpus
obsolescence.
The fifth study comes from the practice of document checking. Gritsay and co-authors bring
the detection of machine fragments within scientific and educational texts to F1 0.968 for Russian
(Gritsay et al. 2023), and their conclusion matches the line of the whole section: a universal
detector for all domains and languages, in their estimate, is hardly achievable.
Russian material is thus covered along the line of text origin and noticeably less so along the
line of the conditions of its creation: none of the five studies varies task mode, regimentation or
channel while origin stays the same. The recurring design limitation is the same as in section 1,
only sharper: the corpus is taken from available sources, and the writing conditions of the human
part are not documented.
Among the included studies, no FPR estimates were found simultaneously for
formal and for translated Russian-language prose.
Summary: what remains unmeasured
The literature measures two things with confidence: the origin of a text, where both the methods
and the limits of their transfer are known, and the result of editing, where expert editing moves a
text away from detection while style after it stays closer to the model than to the author.
In the included sample of 73 studies we found no research that separately assessed
how text production conditions — task mode, regimentation level and generation
channel — change a text’s machine-likeness indicators while its origin stays the same.
Three groups of studies come right up to this point and stop: prevalence estimates count
the share of text that has passed through a model (Liang et al. 2024, Kobak et al. 2025);
hybrid-authorship studies vary the mode of editing but not the conditions of the original writing
(Zeng et al. 2024, Artemova et al. 2025, Su et al. 2025, Baumler et al. 2026); fairness studies
measure the dependence of the score on the writer’s group, not on the conditions under which
the writer wrote (Liang et al. 2023, Stowe et al. 2026, Basu et al. 2026).
The practical stakes are visible from the pair Terčon and Dobrovoljc 2025 versus Fredrick and
Craven 2025: the direction of differences in lexical diversity changes with the composition of the
human control group. If a machine-likeness indicator depends on the conditions under which the
human reference was written, a threshold calibrated on one corpus transfers to another without
guarantees. In accusatory use this means the risk of punishing regimentation rather than the
origin of the text.
Add to this Geng and Trotta 2025: the frequency of words publicly named as characteristic
of the model falls in arXiv abstracts soon after such lists are published — authors adapt. Any
8
conclusion that rests on a marker dictionary has a shelf life, and this is an argument for features
not reducible to a word list.

one cannot claim that the prompt changed — nor the opposite.
A response is described by two independent variables instead of a single validity threshold.
technical_validity covers response integrity: below 50 words a response counts as a technical
failure and is regenerated. length_instruction_compliance describes compliance with the
instruction on a length of 1200–1800 words in five grades fromsevere underlength to severe
overlength. The separation was introduced because the previous single threshold of 480 words

systematically sent P3-mode texts for regeneration and would thereby have biased the primary

2. Identifiability and outcome status
Design identifiability was checked on 27 July 2026 against the registry passports,before the
dependent variable was computed(design-support-table.md).
The check showed that genre in the human subset serves as a proxy for both the regimentation
level and origin. Level 3 is represented by the single genrescience, level 0 by the single genre
prose; for the groups of the primary outcome O3 the genre intersection is empty.
Hence the statuses assigned before computation:
• O1— a pre-specified contrast. The difference between modes P2 and P1 lives inside the
machine subset, where task, genre and channel are fully crossed, and the contrast itself was
registered before data collection.
• O2 and O3— descriptive. The registered estimates for them are not computed, and no
p-values are published.
The study is not a fully confirmatory analysis.The O1 contrast was specified in advance,
but three of the four operationalisations used to estimate it received their operationalisation on
27 July, after the results of the first procedure had been seen (§4). The correct formulation: a pre-
specified contrast estimated predominantly with exploratory operationalisations. Confirmatory
status belongs to the question, not to the whole set of measurement instruments.
The downgrade of O2 and O3 took place before the machine-likeness score was computed and
does not depend on the obtained numbers.

4. Four operationalisations of the machine-likeness score
The effect is tested in four ways so that the conclusion does not reduce to an artefact of a single
implementation. No procedure is designated the reference: sign agreement across all four is an
argument, divergence is a result in its own right.
12
1. Interpretable index.A weighted procedure over countable features, with weights fixed
before data collection. Scale 0–100.
2. Trained classifier.Logistic regression on the primary features, evaluated only on group
holdouts by topic, source, model, task mode, genre, revision family and time. Nested
cross-validation for regularisation; the diagnostic baselines ‘topic only’ and ‘source only’ are
mandatory.
3.Zero-shot metric.Mean NLL under an open Russian-language model.
4. Judge model.The median of three runs with fixed seeds; the dispersion is recorded
separately.
Procedures 2–4 carry exploratory status: their operationalisation was written on 27 July, after
the results of procedure 1 had been seen, whereas the preregistration specified each in a single
line. Procedures 1 and 2 count as one family of evidence: both aggregate the same feature matrix
over the same documents and differ in how the weights are obtained.
5. Estimation of the primary outcome O1
O1 is the difference in machine-likeness score between task modes on pairedtask×model×
replicate observations; two contrasts are registered,P3−P1 and P2−P1 . The unit is points
on the 0–100 scale.
The cluster is the task: forty-five tasks, each containing all channels, modes and replicates.
Confidence intervals are computed by cluster bootstrap over tasks at the 95% level; the absolute
difference with its interval is published, not only a p-value.
The family of primary outcomes is closed by a Bonferroni correction for the two registered
tests,α= 0.025 per test. Sensitivity contrasts are compared against the same threshold.
Benjamini–Hochberg was registered for the feature family and receives the statusnot appli-
cable: no inferential tests of individual features were run, and the four differently scaled summary
contrasts do not form a homogeneous family. The wording was fixed before computation.
Individual features in the figures and the error analysis remain descriptive: effects and intervals
without selection by p-value.

7. Stability stress test
The panel comprises 60 documents selected and fixed before the run (stress-panel-v1.csv).
The panel does not change between revisions.
Of the sixteen registered transformations, ten are executed: em-dash replacement, markup
changes, heading removal, paragraph reordering, punctuation correction, text shortening, text
expansion, adding spelling errors, homoglyph substitution, transfer between formats. Six are not
assessed: five require material absent from the corpus — the A→H branch with editors, machine
rewriting, translation and back-translation, paraphrasing; the sixth, removal of typical clichés,
was ruled infeasible afterprep-v5, because the registered artefact list produces no hits on the
cleaned text.
The panel of 60 documents and 10 transformations yields 600 primary document×
transformation cells. Each cell passes through all four procedures on their native scales:
the index on 0–100, the classifier as the probability of class A, NLL in its own units, the judge on
0–100 with the median of three seeds.
The instability threshold is set separately for each procedure, before the run.A
single threshold cannot be applied: the procedures operate on different scales. Inputs, scales,
directions and thresholds of the four procedures are summarised in Table 2.
The notation is kept distinct:P1, P2 and P3 are task modes(§1.1); the procedures are
referred to by number.
For procedure 2 the two quantities are computed separately, following the aggregation rule
of the amendment: the share of eligible models with|∆p| > 0.05 is the instability, the share

document and then over the 60 documents with equal weight. For procedure 3 the absolute
change cannot be called points, so no share of unstable cases is defined for it at all.
The 5.0 threshold is operational, not empirically validated. Alongside the event share,

8. Versioning and invalidation rules
Each run is described by a manifest with hashes of code, inputs and outputs. An erroneous
manifest is not deleted: it is markedinvalidated with a reason, and work continues in a new
revision. A revision voids the child manifests assembled under the previous parent and forces
their procedures to be recomputed. The final numbers of the main series were obtained under the
sixth revision in a single chain; the stress test stands at revision r5 after three invalidated ones.
Changes to the computation code require an amendment and a new revision. Hashes of code
and inputs are fixed before launch; the chain verifies them and blocks the start on any mismatch.
Code checking takes place before the run, on synthetic data of the same type that production
supplies.
Parse caches are keyed by document_id×input_sha256×cache_version×model_
revision. The preprocessing version is recorded as provenance but is not part of the va-
lidity check: a byte-identical input yields a byte-identical parse. New entries do not overwrite
earlier ones, so earlier series remain reproducible.
The procedure completion gate checks completeness mechanically — the number of cells,
feature coverage, denominators, absence of dropped categories — and stops the chain on a
blocked or incomplete status. A triggered gate counts as a quality control result; loosening the
tolerance to let a run pass is prohibited.
A separate invariant checks cells whose input has not changed: when theprose profile, the
full profile and all preprocessing counters match, the score must equal the baseline identically,
with a tolerance of 1e-9.
5 Results
1. Corpus composition and QA closure
The corpus comprises 1882 documents: 1079 machine and 803 human (Table 1; the data flow
and per-stratum exclusions are in Table S1). The machine part was produced by four generation
channels — each backed by its own model family — across 45 tasks in three task modes with two
replicates. The human part consists of archival texts published before 2022-01-01. The active
preprocessing version isprep-v5; the features are collected infeature-matrix-v5.csv.
Preprocessing removes collection artefacts, and most of its rules apply to the human part:
comment threads in 67 documents totalling 680,570 characters, sidebars and navigation, dangling
lead-ins, paragraph segmentation in 304 documents, typographic hyphenation of the formза-
ключение — 6955 cases in 124 documents, all human:urfu 3460, spbgu 3390, cyberleninka
100, with isolated cases intaiga_magazines, taiga_arzamas and lenta. For the machine part,
the channel wrapper was removed: 99deepseek_pro documents opened with an address to the
client who commissioned the text instead of the article text. The asymmetry of the rules is
acknowledged and recorded: syllable hyphenation is produced by PDF layout, while machine
texts arrive from the API without layout.
Residual verbatim repeats were measured by a symmetric scan of both parts. The threshold
is a share of repeated sentences above 0.10 with a length filter of 40 characters.17 human
documents exceeded the threshold, and none of the 1079 machine documents did;
in the machine part the share of repeats is zero for every document, not merely below the
threshold. On the previous preprocessing version the same ratio was sharper: 97 against zero, so
the correction removed about eighty per cent of the cases.

The seventeen candidates were classified manually by a single rater (Table S9): 10 are

The defects are block-level repeats of fragments in the intermediary datasetburiy_2014 and

O1 compares task modes within the machine corpus. Both halves of a pair were produced by the

The signs disagree, and this is a result in its own right.Three procedures give a

Theplanforthecross-procedurecomparisonandthespecificationsofprocedures2–

comparison, not a fully prospective registration of all four results: by the time the plan was fixed,
the result of the first procedure was already known.
Procedures 1 and 2 count as one family of evidence, not two independent ones: both aggregate
the same matrix over the same documents.
Procedure 2 computes the contrast on a different population — only theseo genre, 120 pairs

3.2. Classifier: ablation, not decomposition
The full and net models are trained separately, so their difference cannot be called a contribution
of structure. Both contrasts appear in Table 4 alongside the quality of the model variants.
The correct wording: excluding the structural features changed the direction of the contrast,
which shows that the result depends on the feature set. Wordings of the form ‘the structural
component contributed so many points’ do not apply to the classifier — they presuppose an
additivity that separately trained models do not have.
Without the structural component the minus disappears in both procedures: for the index
the sign flips, for the classifier the effect ceases to differ from zero.
3.3. Comparing the two runs with each other
A separate question is what each of the two changes between the frozen runs contributed. Both
changed the selected regularisation for three holdouts out of eighteen.
Corpus correction— 34 documents were excluded, and the text changed in
68. The shifts are small in absolute value on average and concentrated on one holdout: AUROC
0.005 on average with a maximum of +0.034, MCC 0.029 with +0.093, FPR 0.021 with
−0.078. The maximum across all six metrics falls onholdout_genre_seo.
Nested CV fix— the corpus is the same; only the inner-split scheme changed: for seven
holdouts the validation became two-class. The shifts are of the same order but with the opposite
sign for SEO: AUROC−0.050, MCC−0.227, FPR +0.234, TPR@1%FPR−0.869.
The wording of the last result is fixed and matters: the degenerate fold happened to select a
stronger regularisation, which worked better on this outer holdout, and this can only be learned
from the test metrics. Writing that it ‘picked C more successfully’ is prohibited — such a wording
would present a defect as an advantage.
The defect predates the correction: seven of the 54 planned inner folds had a single-class
validation already in the first version of the classifier, where the selection of C skipped them
silently — neither the metrics, nor the manifest, nor the report contained the number of folds
used.

8. Manual analysis of unstable cases
Thirty cases were selected by a rule fixed before selection: sorting by the share of models that
flipped their decision, with ties broken by the largest probability shift. The set is 30 cells on 28
documents, with a cutoff share of 0.750.
27
The analysis was blind; the procedure was structural attribution. Hidden were the document
identifier, the origin class, the split, the transformation name, both probabilities, the direction of
the decision flip and the position in the selection; two texts were presented in random order. The
verdict table was fixed by hash before the key was revealed.
The procedure is called blind structural attribution with programmatic text
matching, not manual reading, and this matters for reading the result. Pairs were matched
programmatically — number of sentences, paragraph separators, repeated occurrences, the set
of unique sentences and a line-by-line diff — and the verbatim fragments cited as grounds were
extracted from the differences found. The analysis took two minutes forty-four seconds with a
single rater. This procedure establishes the mechanism of change and makes no claim to semantic
expertise: shifts not visible in structure it does not detect.

The card showed thefull profile.Transformations acting through theprose/full boundary

The composition of the set limits the blinding.Twenty-six of the thirty cases fall on a

was recorded before the analysis began. A separate test for an order effect was not introduced: it
had not been registered.
Two cards were relabelled before the table was fixed.An automatic heuristic attributed

matched the mechanism.
6 Discussion
1. There is no single measurable ‘machine-likeness’
Four procedures assess one corpus on the same documents and disagree in sign. On theP3−P1
contrast, the style index, the classifier and zero-shot NLL give a minus, the judge model a plus,
29
and none of the four intervals covers zero. On the second contrast the signs of procedures 1, 2
and 4 diverge the same way; there the diverging procedure 3 is indistinguishable from zero (p =
0.1988), so its contribution to the disagreement is weaker.
This is not an error in one of the procedures that should be found and fixed. Their inputs
differ: two work with the feature matrix, two with raw text; one has weights fixed before data
collection, another has weights trained on the data. The sign disagreement is a property of the
construct, not a measurement failure.
The direct practical consequence: the question ‘which detector is correct’ is ill-posed. The
better question is what exactly each of them operationalises, and whether their operationalisations
coincide in the application of interest.
They cannot be averaged. Index points, class probability, mean token NLL and a rubric scale
are four different scales, and a pooled effect across them is meaningless. Procedures 1 and 2 are
also not independent: both aggregate the same feature matrix.
2. Corpus assembly conditions are stronger than stylistic signals
The baseline on a single input — the source identifier — reached a median AUROC of 1.0000
across the thirteen holdouts where AUROC is defined, above the model on twenty-two style
features (0.9973). For comparison: the length baseline gives 0.4364, the negative control with
cluster-level shuffled labels 0.3614.
The source identifier turned out to be an almost deterministic proxy for the origin class. The
corpus itself is built that way: human texts were collected from platforms, machine texts were
produced by generation channels, and no source supplies both classes.
The practical limit this imposes is hard. The assessment does not transfer to sources where
human and machine texts coexist — and that is exactly the situation detectors are applied to:
one platform, one section, a mixed stream. High metrics on group holdouts do not justify such
transfer.
3. The classifier learns corpus mechanisms, not only style
The stress test produced a chain in which every link is measured.
Verbatim sentence repeats in the corpus occur only in human texts: 17 human documents
exceeded the 0.10 threshold and none of the 1079 machine documents did, and in the machine part
the repeat share is zero for every document. Of the seventeen candidates, ten turned out to be
extraction defects, four were legitimate properties of the source, and three remained unresolved.
Feature M01 — the standard deviation of cosine similarity between adjacent sentences —
is systematically higher in human texts: median 0.0907 versus 0.0746, Cliff’sδ =−0.78. Its
coefficient is negative in all eighteen models, with a median rank of 4 out of 22 by absolute value.
Sentence duplication raises this quantity by 7.86σ and shifts the decision towards the ‘human’
class. All 109 decision flips under text expansion go in one direction.
The mechanism is corpus- and model-specific. It explains the behaviour of this classifier on
this corpus and does not make verbatim repetition a marker of human writing: the association
arose because repeats in the training data came from one side, mostly as a trace of collection.
4. Preprocessing is part of the measurement instrument
The nemotron_b016_P1_r1 case shows this in pure form. Removing emphasis markers did not
change the full profile by a single character — 14,485 characters byte-for-byte — but changed
the distribution of text between headings andprose, the values of the format counters and the
classifier’s input vector. In all six applicable models, a confident machine decision flipped to
human: the probability dropped from 0.97–1.00 to 0.03–0.23.
30
The mechanism is traceable: lines previously recognised as bold headings stop being headings
and move into the prose profile.prose grows from 5540 to 5792 characters, the emphasis counter
falls from 76 to zero.
So the model reacts not only to the words of the text but also to preprocessing decisions
about which fragments count as prose. The data preparation pipeline enters the measurement
instrument on a par with the model, and its version must be published and pinned the same way
as the weights.
The case was found by blind structural attribution: the card showed thefull profile, where
therearenodifferences, andtheverdict‘theinterventionshowednoeffect’wasacorrectobservation
alongside a decision flip of 0.97.
5. Formal register produces a systematic bias, but this is not demographic
unfairness
The registered subgroup disparity rule fired for theHH-formal-register subgroup in 5 of 18
holdouts: the subgroup’s median FPR is 0.066, with a difference of 0.043 from the rest of the test
set. The aggregate fairness verdict did not change, but the effect stopped being a property of a
single platform.
Calling this demographic unfairness is wrong, and the reason is not caution of wording. The
subgroup coincides with thescience genre document for document — 167 texts — so register
and genre are not separable on this corpus. The group is defined by text type, not by a property
of people.
This coincidence is not isolated. All three hard-human labels reproduce a genre in full:
HH-formal-register — 167 science texts, HH-polished — 152 news, HH-translation — 73
translation. The annotation was conceived as a layer on top of the corpus, but in fact it
repeated the corpus’s genre partition, and separate estimation of register and genre effects on
these data is impossible in principle, not for lack of power. Separating them requires a corpus
where the formal register occurs in several genres — no such sample exists in this study.
The correct name is an error asymmetry by register. It means that the cost of error is borne
by authors of formal scientific texts, but it does not permit claims about groups of people.
6. Good averages hide genre heterogeneity
Median AUROC of the main model 0.9973, median MCC 0.9338, median FPR 0.0380 — by
average metrics the classifier looks almost error-free.
The spread says otherwise. Forholdout_genre_seo, AUROC is 0.9433, MCC 0.6560, FPR
0.3098, TPR@5%FPR 0.6250 against the median 0.9833. The same holdout is the worst in
calibration: Brier 0.1796, ECE 0.1973, MCE 0.7548.
The coincidence is not accidental and it matters: SEO is problematic in separation quality
and in calibration at once. Publishing one averaged figure for such a profile means hiding that in
one genre holdout out of seven every third human text receives a machine verdict.
The genre label also hides the spread between platforms within a genre: in thenews genre
the median FPR is 0.013, while across the platforms inside it — 0.033, 0.000 and 0.000.
7. Abstaining from low-confidence decisions reduces risk, but no operating
point has been chosen
The risk–coverage curve shows the expected: error falls from 0.0468 at full coverage to 0.0059 at
half coverage. Abstaining from the least confident decisions works.
The threshold, however, has not been chosen deliberately. There is no justified number, and
setting one after seeing the results would mean fitting it to the answer. The curve describes the
system’s behaviour and is not a deployment recommendation.
31
Separately: the abstention policy registered for the instrument is formulated in terms of
properties of the input text — length, genre, conflicting signals — not the predicted probability.
The measured coverage does not account for these abstentions.
Overall calibration is, at that, acceptable — macro Brier 0.0365, ECE robust to the number
of bins in the range 0.0557–0.0581 — but stability of the estimate is not proof of good calibration,
and the heterogeneity from point 6 does not go away.
8. Practical conclusion
The detector assesses a combination of text, source, formatting and pipeline, not biological or
creative authorship directly.
The study establishes this through four measured facts: the source separates the classes better
than style (point 2), the classifier learned the corpus trace of repeats (point 3), a change of
formatting without a change of text flips the decision (point 4), quality and calibration depend
sharply on genre (point 6).
For application this means the following. A ‘machine text’ verdict is not a claim about
authorship and cannot serve as grounds for accusation. An assessment obtained on one corpus
does not transfer to a stream where both classes come from one source. A change of publication
template or of the text preparation pipeline changes the verdict without the author’s involvement.
None of these conclusions requires assuming that detectors are useless. They require assuming
that a detector measures text production conditions together with the text itself — which was
the study’s original hypothesis.
9. Limitations and future validation
The main limitation is named in point 2:the classes share no sources.Because sources do
not overlap between classes, the separate effects of text origin and platform are not identifiable
on these data without additional untestable assumptions. Future work must include sources that
supply both classes.
Transformations must be validated on a separate development panel.The attempt to
register shortening without duplication showed why: the transformation was rejected at preflight
by a criterion that itself proved ill-matched to the nature of the quantity, and choosing a threshold
after seeing the result is inadmissible. The panel for validating criteria must be separated from
the panel on which the result is computed.
An external corpus is mandatory.All conclusions were obtained on one corpus of one
language, collected by one procedure. Transfer to another language, another domain or another
generation of models has not been tested.
The operationalisation of preprocessing must be stable and published.Point 4
shows that the decision about the boundary between heading and prose changes the verdict.
Hence the pipeline version is part of the method description, and comparing detectors without
pinning the preprocessing is invalid.
In addition, the known and recorded limitations remain: mixed-effects models did not yield
interpretable estimates for three different reasons, the volume transformations turned out to be
composite, three repeat cases remained unresolved, and candidate classification was carried out
by a single rater.

Ethics statement
Ethical approval was not required. The study works with published texts: the human part of the
corpus is drawn from open sources, and six documents come from the author’s personal archive
that predates the study. No experiments on human subjects were conducted, and personal data
neither enter the analysis nor are published. Recruitment of participants who would contribute
texts under written consent was planned, and the consent forms and the registry are published
in the repository, but the recruitment did not take place: no text from an external participant
entered the corpus and no payments were made.
The author treats the risk of applying the results as the main ethical question of this work.
Detectors of machine-generated text are used to support accusations, and a type I error costs
a person a degree or a payment while that person has no systematic way to prove authorship.
The study therefore reports the false positive rate separately for vulnerable subgroups instead
of average accuracy alone, describes refusal to issue a verdict as a normal operating mode, and
states directly that none of the four procedures is fit to serve as the sole basis for an accusation.
Dual use.The features by which a text is recognised as machine-generated also help to
disguise it. The study does not optimise detection evasion and publishes no evasion pipeline; the
text transformations are set up as robustness stress tests and described as such. The work draws
no conclusions about the language competence, education or origin of the authors.

5. The parse cache key
The Stanza, NER, and embedding caches were addressed bydocument_id alone. During the
prep-v5 correction, the text of 68 documents changed while their identifiers stayed the same, and
the cache would have returned a stale parse for them.
The key was switched to document_id×input_sha256×cache_version×model_
revision. The preprocessing version is recorded as provenance but does not enter the va-
lidity check: a byte-identical input yields a byte-identical parse, and requiring the version to
match would have forced recomputation of 1814 documents with no change in the result. The
premise was verified — for all 1916 documents, theprose_sha256 of the version under which the
parse was built matched theprose_sha256in prep-v4.
New entries do not overwrite old ones: the name contains the input hash, so the prep-v4 parse
stays on disk and series v1 remains reproducible.

9. Explaining the effect of transformations 10 and 11 through volume — a
withdrawn interpretation
Status: withdrawn interpretation.The wording is given in full because for a week it served
as the basis for reading the result, and the entries of 31 July and 1 August referred to it. It has
no force in Results or in the main conclusion.
The withdrawn wording: ‘the classifier is robust to cosmetic edits and collapses under volume
change; expanding the text flips the decision in nearly half of the cases, shortening — in one and
a half percent, with high probability instability’.
What refutes it. Shortening reduces volume by 24%, expansion increases it by 25% —
symmetric changes. Yet the decision flips number 4 of 464 versus 109 of 464, a 27-fold difference.
It is shortening that loses more paragraphs: a median of 83.5% against 75.6%. Neither length nor
loss of markup explains such asymmetry.

Construct and design
1. The index covers a small part of the stated construct.Of thirteen categories, two
are fully operationalised, five partially, four are not operationalised, one is reported as a
separate output, one is absent. The total weight of the non-operationalised categories in the
‘Mixed’ domain is 0.23 out of 1.00, and it has not been redistributed among the rest. All
four share one reason: the source formulates the mechanism as a narrowing of dispersion,
while the features measure level. Source:construct-coverage.md.
2. Two of the three primary outcomes are unidentifiable.In the human part
regulation_level coincides withgenre one to one; for the O3 groups the genre intersection
is empty. O2 and O3 were reclassified as descriptive before the dependent variable was
computed; no p-values were calculated for them. Source:design-support-table.md.
3. Procedures 2–4 have exploratory status.Their operationalisation was written on 27
July, after the results of procedure 1 had been seen; §7 of the preregistration specified each
in a single line. Source:procedures-2-4-registration.md§2.
4. Procedures 1 and 2 are one family of evidence.Both aggregate the same feature
matrix on the same documents and differ in how the weights are obtained. Their agreement
counts as one source, not two.
5. All four procedures share one corpus.These are alternative measurements on the
same documents, not independent samples: shared selection biases, genres, time slice and
collection defects. Genuine replication would require a bridge set; its requirements are in
design-support-table.md§5.
6. The net estimand cannot be operationalised for procedures 3 and 4.Removing
markup is not the same as subtracting the structure prescribed by the brief: the brief also
dictates the order of exposition, the set of sections and the mandatory points. No imitation
was created for the sake of table symmetry.
Magnitude and precision
7. All effects are small.Standardised paired effects of procedure 1: |d_z| from 0.143 to
0.305. The mean differences−1.77 and +3.28 are points of the operationalised index, not
percentages of machine-likeness.
8. The judge’s resolution is below the effect size.15 unique values of the three-run
median across 1882 documents, 85.9% of scores at two points of the scale, ICC(2,1) = 0.477,
mean range across three runs 4.78 against an SD of 3.26 for the median machine-part scores.
9. The intervals are unadjusted, 95%.Bootstrap replicates were not saved; 97.5%
intervals cannot be extracted from the run. Decisions are made by p againstα = 0.025
after Bonferroni. The sensitivity contrast O1-netP2−P1 does not pass the threshold (p
= 0.0416) and is not called significant.
57
10. The 120 classifier pairs are 15 task clusters.The P2b contrast was computed on a
different population than the rest: only the seo genre against 45 clusters for procedures 1, 3
and 4.
11. Four machine source clusters give a weak estimate of cross-channel generalis-
ability— this limitation is published at every mention of the external P2b folds.
Diagnostics that did not work
12. A cluster-level negative control is not feasible in this design.Four machine clusters
against 124 human ones give a bimodal distribution over twenty permutations (mean
AUROC 0.132–0.434 with a spread of 0.001–0.998); the mean is not a null hypothesis.
Replacing the cluster permutation with a document permutation would bring the leakage
back. The diagnostic is recorded as not feasible.

Limitations of measurement and construct validity
22. Hard tercile discretisation amplifies small numerical changes near the boundaries.
tercile_score cuts the rank at 1/3 and 2/3, so an input that changed slightly produces a
stepwise jump in the index. Confirmed by the computational reproducibility diagnostic:
in the 30 July measurement, out of 1853 checks of features M01, M02 and M05 over the
panel, 30 values lie within one rank step of a boundary and 9 exactly on it (status-2026-
07-30.md). This is not an estimate of the effect of the excluded transformation t14.
23. Format counters are normalised to volume.R06, R07 and F01 are computed per
1000 words of thefull profile, P01 as the share of the profile outside connected prose.
A change in text length can therefore shift the format score without any change in the
absolute number of markup elements. The limitation concerns the operationalisation of
the features itself and holds regardless of which transformations entered the final set. The
specific numbers on which the mechanism was discovered are moved to the reproducibility
appendix with the label invalidated diagnostic run: they were obtained on a run with a
transformation recognised as defective and are not a stress-test result.
