\begin{abstract}
Detectors of machine-generated Russian text are trained on corpora built with 2020--2022 generators, while the
texts they meet today come from much newer models. We measure how such detectors transfer across corpora and
generator eras. Encoders fine-tuned on CoAT, zero-shot Binoculars with Russian-capable model pairs, and encoders
trained on corpus mixtures are evaluated on CoAT, LLMTrace, AINL-Eval 2025 and two new test sets generated with
T-pro~2.0 and Qwen3.8-27B. ruRoBERTa-large trained on CoAT drops from 0.95 AUROC on CoAT to 0.52--0.64 on
the newer corpora, and encoders trained on modern corpora score CoAT machine texts below human ones
(0.46--0.48 AUROC). With Russian-capable model pairs, Binoculars separates free continuations from human text
with up to 0.84 AUROC and paraphrases with at most 0.64; adding its features to a fine-tuned encoder changes
AUROC by less than 0.04. Mixtures that include LLMTrace reach 0.79--0.82 AUROC on the 2026 test sets, but the
training and test corpora share generator families, so the gain cannot be attributed to corpus diversity
alone. Code:
\url{https://github.com/egorsorvin/ru-ai-text-detection}.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
Detectors of AI-generated text are used in education, content moderation and fraud screening. For Russian, the
main public resources come from the RuATD 2022 shared task and its extension CoAT~\cite{shamardina2022ruatd,
shamardina2025coat}, built with generators such as ruGPT-3 and ruT5. Generators have changed since then: current
models write fluent text, and a detector that learned the artifacts of older models may miss it. In English,
detection is known to degrade on unseen generators and domains~\cite{dugan2024raid}, but this has not been
measured across Russian corpora of different eras.

We evaluate three kinds of detectors on three public corpora, CoAT, LLMTrace~\cite{tolstykh2025llmtrace} and
AINL-Eval~\cite{batura2025ainl}, under a leave-one-corpus-out protocol. We also build two test sets that pair
CoAT human texts with outputs of 2026 generators, so the human side stays fixed and only the generator changes.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a cross-corpus and cross-era evaluation of Russian detectors, including two new held-out test sets;
    \item evidence that the shift goes both ways: ruRoBERTa-large trained on CoAT flags 14--17\% of machine
    texts from 2026 generators, while encoders trained on modern corpora score ruGPT-3 outputs from CoAT as more
    human than human texts (0.32--0.38 AUROC);
    \item an analysis of Binoculars~\cite{hans2024binoculars} for Russian: with Russian-capable pairs it separates
    free continuation from human text with 0.69--0.84 AUROC and paraphrase or simplification with 0.51--0.66,
    and a threshold fitted on CoAT gives 0.50--0.63 balanced accuracy on newer corpora;
    \item a negative result: adding Binoculars features to a multi-corpus encoder changes AUROC by $-0.03$ to
    $+0.03$ in a single run, and the gains from corpus mixtures are confounded with generator families shared
    between training and test corpora.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Russian corpora.} RuATD 2022~\cite{shamardina2022ruatd} and its extension
CoAT~\cite{shamardina2025coat} contain texts from 13 generators of 2020--2021 fine-tuned for translation,
paraphrasing, summarization and simplification, plus free generation. LLMTrace~\cite{tolstykh2025llmtrace}
covers generators of 2023--2025, including the GPT-4 family, GigaChat, YandexGPT and Qwen.
AINL-Eval 2025~\cite{batura2025ainl} contains scientific abstracts by GPT-4-Turbo, Gemma-2-27B, Llama-3.3-70B and
withheld models. Each work evaluates detectors within its own corpus.

\paragraph{Generalization.} M4~\cite{wang2024m4}, MULTITuDE~\cite{macko2023multitude} and
RAID~\cite{dugan2024raid} show that trained detectors lose accuracy on unseen generators and domains.
Kirichenko~\cite{kirichenko2026sameorigin} shows that on Russian data detector scores also follow the text
source, so cross-corpus results mix a generator shift with a source shift.

\paragraph{Zero-shot detection.} DetectGPT~\cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}, Fast-DetectGPT~\cite{bao2024fastdetectgpt}
and Binoculars~\cite{hans2024binoculars} use the probabilities of a language model instead of training.
Binoculars detects over 90\% of ChatGPT texts at a 0.01\% false-positive rate in English, but LLMTrace reports an
AI-class F1 of 0.02 on Russian. Paraphrasing evades such detectors~\cite{krishna2023paraphrasing}.

\section{Model Description}
We compare three detectors.

\paragraph{Fine-tuned encoder.} The \texttt{[CLS]} vector $h$ of a pretrained encoder (ruBERT-base or
ruRoBERTa-large) goes through dropout and a linear layer to two logits. All weights are trained with
cross-entropy.

\paragraph{Binoculars.} For a text $s = x_1 \dots x_L$, an observer model $O$ (base) and a performer model $P$
(instruct version of the same family):
$$\ell_P(s) = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i} \log p_P(x_i \mid x_{<i}), \qquad
\ell_X(s) = -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i} \sum_{v} p_O(v \mid x_{<i}) \log p_P(v \mid x_{<i}),$$
$$B(s) = \ell_P(s) / \ell_X(s).$$
$\ell_P$ is the log-perplexity of the text, $\ell_X$ the expected log-perplexity of a typical text in the same
contexts. A low $B$ indicates machine text. Nothing is trained. We use three pairs: Qwen3-4B, YandexGPT-5-Lite-8B
and Qwen3-14B.

\paragraph{Encoder with Binoculars features.} The vector $z = (B, \log\ell_P, \log\ell_X)$ from the Qwen3-14B
pair is standardized on the training data, projected by a linear layer with GELU to 32 dimensions and
concatenated with $h$ before the classification layer. The encoder and both layers are trained; the Binoculars
models stay frozen.

\paragraph{Training on corpus mixtures.} The fine-tuned encoder and the encoder with Binoculars features are also
trained on balanced samples of 16,000 texts per corpus from two or three corpora and tested on the corpus left
out.

\paragraph{Public corpora.} CoAT (\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/RussianNLP/coat}{RussianNLP/coat},
Apache-2.0): the official test labels are closed, so the HF validation split is our test and 10\% of train,
stratified by generator, is our dev. LLMTrace
(\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/iitolstykh/LLMTrace_detection}{iitolstykh/LLMTrace\_detection}): Russian
part, official splits, texts labelled \emph{mixed} removed. AINL-Eval
(\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/iis-research-team/AINL-Eval-2025}{iis-research-team/AINL-Eval-2025},
Apache-2.0): the official test labels are closed, so \texttt{dev\_full} is our test and 5\% of train is our dev;
one empty text is removed.

\paragraph{Our test sets.} We sampled 1,000 human texts of 15--200 words from the CoAT test split per task and
generated one machine text per source with T-pro~2.0 (32B) and Qwen3.8-27B, reasoning mode off, temperature 0.7,
top-$p$ 0.9. The tasks are \emph{continue} (the model continues the first 10 words of the human text),
\emph{paraphrase} and \emph{simplify}. Outputs are cleaned by fixed rules: removal of chat preambles, markdown,
reasoning blocks and repeated prompt words; rejection of refusals, non-Russian outputs, repetition loops and
copies of the source. Continuations are cut at a sentence boundary to at most 1.2 times the source length. The
rules rejected 6 of 3,000 T-pro outputs and 8 of 3,000 Qwen outputs. Each test set contains the machine texts
and their human sources. Median length is 23 words for human texts and 21--32 words for machine texts, depending
on the task. The sets are produced by \texttt{scripts/generate\_set.py}; raw outputs are stored with the cleaned
texts.


\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Metrics}
The main metric is AUROC, the probability that a random machine text gets a higher machine score than a random
human text. It does not need a threshold and is not affected by class imbalance. We also report balanced
accuracy, the mean of the true positive and true negative rates, and the true positive rate at a 5\% false
positive rate:
$$\mathrm{TPR@5\%} = \Pr\big(f(s) > t_{0.95} \mid s \text{ is machine}\big),$$
where $t_{0.95}$ is the 95th percentile of the scores $f$ of human test texts.

\subsection{Experiment Setup}
Encoders are trained for 2 epochs with AdamW, weight decay 0.01, linear warmup over 6\% of steps, gradient
clipping at 1.0, bf16 and at most 256 tokens. The checkpoint with the best dev accuracy is evaluated once on
every test set. ruBERT-base uses batch 32 and learning rate $2\cdot10^{-5}$; ruRoBERTa-large uses batch 16 and
$10^{-5}$. Binoculars uses bf16 and at most 256 tokens; its accuracy threshold is fitted on CoAT dev. All results
come from one run with seed 42. CoAT baselines were trained on an RTX 3060, the rest on one rented H100 80GB in
about 3.5 hours of instance time. Code and exact commands are in the repository.

\subsection{Baselines}
TF-IDF over word 1--2-grams and character 2--5-grams, 300k features each, with logistic regression; ruBERT-base
and ruRoBERTa-large fine-tuned on CoAT. Zero-shot Binoculars serves as the training-free baseline.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Old to new.} Detectors trained on CoAT reach 0.43--0.71 AUROC on the newer corpora and
    0.55--0.62 on our sets. On our sets the human texts come from CoAT, so only the generator differs:
    ruRoBERTa-large keeps 93\% of human texts correct but flags 14--17\% of machine texts.
    \item \textbf{New to old.} Trained on LLMTrace and AINL, the encoder scores CoAT machine texts below human
    ones (0.46). By generator, ruGPT-3 outputs get 0.32--0.38 and translation systems 0.64--0.70. Binoculars shows
    the same pattern (0.40--0.44 overall). ruGPT-3 texts are about twice as long as the human texts, so length
    does not explain the inversion.
    \item \textbf{Mixtures.} Adding LLMTrace to CoAT raises AUROC on AINL from 0.64 to 0.91; adding AINL raises
    LLMTrace from 0.52 to 0.67. Mixtures with LLMTrace reach 0.79--0.82 on our sets. LLMTrace training data
    contains the GPT-4 family (37\% of its machine texts), Llama-3.3, Gemma-2 and Qwen models, which also produced
    AINL and our sets, so these gains may come from covering the test generator families.
    \item \textbf{Binoculars features} change AUROC by $-0.03$ to $+0.03$, within run-to-run noise.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Thresholds.} With a threshold fitted on CoAT dev, Binoculars has 0.50--0.63 balanced accuracy on the
newer corpora. A threshold fitted on the dev sets of the other modern corpora gives 0.57--0.72 on LLMTrace and
AINL, within 0.04 of a threshold fitted on the test corpus itself. At a 5\% false positive rate, the all-corpora
encoder catches 92--95\% of machine texts on LLMTrace and AINL, which it saw in training, and 36--38\% on our
sets.

\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated Russian AI-text detectors across three public corpora and two test sets generated with 2026 models.
Detectors trained on 2020--2021 generators lose most of their signal on modern text, and detectors trained on
modern text are inverted on the old corpus. Binoculars with Russian-capable pairs separates free continuations
(up to 0.84 AUROC) but not paraphrases, and its features do not help a fine-tuned encoder. Training on corpus
mixtures gives the best results on our test sets, 0.79--0.82 AUROC, but the corpora share generator families, and
at a 5\% false positive rate the best models catch under 45\% of machine texts from the new generators. Open
questions are a split that holds out whole generator families, several seeds, and generators outside the Qwen
lineage.
